SCIENTIFIC STARTING POINTS

Maintain four competing explanations:

1. Representation collisions:
States with different useful futures compete under the same archive identity. Metroid’s equal-count/different-capability states are a concrete lead.

First probe: capture a bounded sample of replacement pairs and execute the same sampled action suffixes from both states. Look for useful continuations produced by the discarded state that the survivor misses under equal probe work. Simple endpoint differences alone are insufficient, and matching finite probes do not prove equivalence.

2. Destructive retention:
One representative loses useful alternatives even after capability identity is corrected.

Compare the existing representative rule with small bounded sets preserving resource tradeoffs or distinct observed continuation behavior. Resource dominance does not prove behavioral dominance. Keep total memory equal and account for the cost of retaining alternatives.

3. Selection starvation:
Useful alternatives survive but receive too little exploration.

Trace discovered → admitted → selectable → selected → extended. Measure exposure before replacement or eviction. If states already survive but are rarely sampled, adding representatives may worsen dilution; test selection separately.

4. Ineffective local exploration:
The search reaches useful states but its action generation rarely advances from them.

From development-discovered states, compare ordinary suffix sampling with narrowly varied action durations or campaign-learned continuations at equal frame work. Keep these diagnostic starts separate from fresh-genesis validation.

Keep at most two implementation candidate families active at once. Choose experiments that distinguish these explanations before expanding the search space of algorithms.
